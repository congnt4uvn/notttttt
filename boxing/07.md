# DAY 6 — Mastering the Cross

Today we develop the second fundamental punch in boxing: the Cross (Punch No. 2).

The cross is a straight punch delivered with your rear hand. It is commonly used after the jab and can generate substantial power through coordinated movement of the legs, hips, torso, and arm.

Your objective is to learn how to deliver the cross without losing balance, dropping your guard, or over-rotating.

The cross is your rear-hand straight punch.

- Orthodox stance: Right-hand cross.
- Southpaw stance: Left-hand cross.

The cross is usually more powerful than the jab because the rear side of your body can contribute more rotational movement and force.

However, power depends on your mechanics, timing, physical development, and distance.

A technically sound cross coordinates several body parts:

| Body part | Function                      |
| --------- | ----------------------------- |
| Rear foot | Allows natural pivot          |
| Rear leg  | Supports force production     |
| Hips      | Rotate toward the target      |
| Torso     | Transfers rotational movement |
| Shoulder  | Moves with the punching arm   |
| Arm       | Extends the punch             |
| Wrist     | Maintains alignment           |

Think of the punch as a coordinated movement rather than an arm-only action.

### Step 1 — Starting position

- Begin in your boxing stance.
- Keep your lead hand near your cheek.
- Keep your rear hand protecting your face.
- Maintain relaxed shoulders.
- Keep your knees slightly bent.

### Step 2 — Rear-foot pivot

Allow your rear foot to rotate naturally as your hips turn.

Do not force your knee or ankle into an uncomfortable position.

### Step 3 — Hip rotation

Rotate your hips toward the target.

The movement should be coordinated with your torso.

### Step 4 — Shoulder rotation

Your rear shoulder moves forward as the rear hand extends.

Your lead shoulder naturally moves back.

### Step 5 — Arm extension

Extend your rear arm along a straight path.

Maintain wrist alignment and avoid locking the elbow.

### Step 6 — Impact position

Your body should remain balanced.

Your chin stays protected and your lead hand stays near your face.

### Step 7 — Recovery

Return your rear hand to your guard.

Restore your original stance and be ready to move.
